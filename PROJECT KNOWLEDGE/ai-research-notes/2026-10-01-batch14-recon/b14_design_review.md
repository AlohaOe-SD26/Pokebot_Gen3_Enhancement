# 2026-10-01 — design v2 step 14: the DESIGN's adversarial review (four angles), verbatim

⚠ **A NOTE, NOT TRUTH.** Four independent read-only reviewers (workflow `step14-design-review`) of the first draft of `2026-10-01-batch14-DESIGN.md`. Their line numbers cite THAT draft. How each finding was folded is the DESIGN's §9.

## Angle `stats`

**Verdict.** M-8 can be run as designed. It is concurrent, starter-balanced and has one shared deadline, and in my simulation its clustered, lane-stratified bootstrap gives close to nominal coverage. The problem is that the pre-registered statistics in §5-§6 would not mean what they say. The most important problem is the acceptance metric C2-C4. It is a first-attempt rate measured only on runs that reached each trainer, and it gives equal weight to the late bosses that only 46-82% of runs reach. In the baseline, Juan's first-attempt win rate is .63 for runs with few earlier leader losses and .11 for the rest. So any arm effect on which runs reach a trainer (honest stops, OPENFENCE's catching, derails, run length) can turn the verdict either way. This has to be fixed in the pre-registration before the go; it needs no change to the batch itself. Three secondary problems compound it. Power is overstated: P(NOT LOWER) for leaders is .64 when the arms are truly equal, and about .45 after Holm. Trainers already at ceiling dilute the margin: a .08 margin on the rivals mean allows a .16-.24 drop at the only rival fights that can be lost. And C5's power figures are about 1.5-2x too high under the design's own clustered test.

### S1 [BLOCKER, VERIFIED]

- **Design:** "C2-C4, the acceptance metric ... The per-trainer FIRST-attempt win rate, with a Draw counted as a loss. Each group takes the equal-weight mean over the trainers that at least 5 runs of EACH arm met." (2026-10-01-batch14-DESIGN.md:181-182)
- **Evidence:** Computed with scratchpad/s14review/reach.py and inline scripts on s14power/runstats.json (the recon's per-run data; its classification is INFERRED, the counts are VERIFIED). (1) Share of baseline runs that ever meet each trainer: Juan .58/.46, Tate & Liza .66/.54, Winona .82/.57, Wally in Victory Road .42/.34 (batch0926/batch0927). (2) Among the 38 runs that met Juan, Juan first-attempt win is .63 for runs with at most the median number of earlier leader losses and .11 for runs above it (19 vs 19). (3) Runs that met Norman but never Tate & Liza averaged 5.71 attempts per early leader (Wattson to Winona); runs that reached Tate & Liza averaged 3.80. (4) The equal-weight mean gives Winona, Tate & Liza and Juan 3/8 of C2.
- **Problem:** Truncation by death: the metric conditions on an event that happens after treatment (reaching the trainer). If B lets weak runs survive Winona instead of stopping, those runs reach Tate & Liza and Juan and mostly lose their first attempt, so B reads LOWER while doing better. If B loses weak runs earlier (a B-only derail, a frame cap, slower runs cut at the deadline), B reads NOT LOWER or HIGHER while doing worse. OPENFENCE is known to change trajectories (K3), so different reach between arms is plausible, not hypothetical. The rule 'at least 5 runs of EACH arm' also makes the trainer set depend on the data.
- **Fix:** Fix the pre-registration before the go (no batch change is needed). Make the confirmatory C2-C4 one of these: (a) the mean over trainers that about 85% or more of baseline runs reach (Roxanne, Brawly, Wattson, Flannery; rival at Route 103, rival in Rustboro, rival on Route 110, Wally in Mauville; Tabitha and Maxie at Mt. Chimney), with the later trainers descriptive; or (b) an unconditional per-run composite: 'beat X on the first try' over runs not cut by the deadline before X, where failing to reach X for any reason other than the deadline counts as a failure. Print a reach check beside every conditional reading (meet rate B vs A per trainer, with a CI). Fix the trainer set from the observed data and hold it fixed inside the bootstrap.

### S2 [SHOULD-FIX, VERIFIED]

- **Design:** "δ = .10 for leaders is about the smallest margin this batch supports." (:226), with the rule "NOT LOWER if the CI's lower bound is above −δ" on "a two-sided 95% percentile CI" (:159, :187), and "A group below its MDD is descriptive only." (:162)
- **Evidence:** The recon's NI column is one-sided α=.05: s14power/power.py:2 says "one-sided non-inferiority MDD80 = 2.49 * SD", and b14_recon_stats.md:50 gives leaders NI ".10". The design's rule is a one-sided α=.025 test, so 80% power needs δ ≥ 2.80×SD, which is the MDD itself. Re-derived MDD for the equal-weight estimand at 31 runs per arm (s14review/mdd.py): leaders .10-.14, rivals .07-.08, Magma/Aqua .06-.09. Without the Space Center multi, as C4 requires (mdd_noSC.py): Magma/Aqua .06-.10, and .087-.100 on batch0927. A simulation of the design's exact procedure (s14review/coverage.py: 300 synthetic experiments from the pooled baseline runs, clusters by (lane, PID), 600-draw lane-stratified percentile bootstrap) gives P(NOT LOWER | arms truly equal) = .640 leaders, .810 rivals, .853 Magma/Aqua, before Holm. At Holm's first step (a 99% CI): Φ(.10/.041 − 2.576) ≈ .45 for leaders.
- **Problem:** The supportable-margin claim is wrong under the design's own test. With zero true difference, leaders read NOT LOWER only 45-64% of the time, so INCONCLUSIVE is the most likely leaders outcome. Line 162 is also ambiguous. If 'below its MDD' means MDD > δ, then by the design's own §6 figures (leaders MDD .11-.12 > δ .10) C2 is descriptive by construction. If it means the observed effect is below the MDD, that is the post-hoc power fallacy, and it would void every NOT LOWER.
- **Fix:** State the power of NOT LOWER at Δ=0 under the actual rule, before and after Holm (about .45-.64 leaders, .66-.81 rivals). Then either accept leaders as likely INCONCLUSIVE in the headline caveat, or widen δ for leaders to about .13-.14, or add lanes. Redefine line 162 as 'if the MDD computed from the realized n exceeds δ, the group's verdict is reported as underpowered', computed from n and never from the observed effect.

- **Evidence:** Baseline first-attempt win, batch0926/batch0927 (s14review/reach.py). ### S5 [SHOULD-FIX, VERIFIED]

- **Design:** "Holm's correction applies across the five CONFIRMATORY readings, C1-C5." (:160); "NOT LOWER if the CI's lower bound is above −δ; LOWER if its upper bound is below 0" (:187-188); "Superiority is reported too: HIGHER" (:190); "REGRESSION if the CI's lower bound is above 0" (:193)
- **Evidence:** Every verdict rule is written on the unadjusted 95% CI (:159), and no p-value is defined for any C. C2-C4 each make up to three claims (NOT LOWER, LOWER, HIGHER), so the family is not five. C5 power under the design's clustered test (s14review/c5power2.py): .44 at f=.5 and .20 at f=.3 with the 95% CI; .23 and .08 with the 99% CI of Holm's first step.
- **Problem:** As written, Holm is a no-op, because the CI rules never change. If it is applied, the CI level each verdict uses depends on how unrelated p-values happen to rank. Putting harm readings (LOWER, C5 REGRESSION) inside a family-wise error family weakens harm detection, which is exactly what the design calls 'the question the batch answers'. C1, a manipulation check, sits in the same family instead of gating C2-C4, although a NOT LOWER when C1 fails says nothing about the package's mechanism.
- **Fix:** Define a bootstrap p for each hypothesis (for NI, the share of resamples at or below −δ) and state the adjusted CI level behind each verdict. Order the confirmatory claims: C1 first as a gatekeeper, then NOT LOWER for C2-C4 by Holm or a fixed sequence. Read the harm signals (C5 REGRESSION and any LOWER) unadjusted, one-sided α=.05, as flags. HIGHER after NI on the same CI is a valid closed-testing step and needs no extra alpha.

### S6 [SHOULD-FIX, INFERRED]

- **Design:** "Its absence is NOT 'no harm': power is .69-.86 if half the ordinary item battles turn into losses, and .34-.49 at 30%." (:195); "Expected: B ≥ A, because the items are removed there." (:193)
- **Evidence:** The source is a per-battle "Poisson approximation, INFERRED" (b14_recon_stats.md:98-103). B's extra losses would fall where the ordinary item battles are, and those are concentrated in a few runs: per-run ordinary item battles have var/mean 3.88 (0926) and 4.76 (0927), computed from runstats.json. Losses themselves are near-Poisson (var/mean 1.05-1.43). I simulated the design's own analysis (s14review/c5power2.py): (lane, PID) clusters, lane-stratified percentile bootstrap, frames as exposure, B's extra losses drawn as Binomial(the run's item battles, f). Power is .44 at f=.5 and .20 at f=.3, with a null false-REGRESSION rate of .007. The inputs are VERIFIED; the simulation uses a proxy denominator (frames, not trainer battles), hence INFERRED.
- **Problem:** The caveat's power numbers are about 1.5-2x too high under the design's own clustered test, and with Holm they fall to about .23 and .08. The expected direction is attributed to KEYITEMS alone, but OPENFENCE, CATCHTRAIN and the other package flags change teams, so the sign of the package effect is not fixed. There is also no verdict word for B < A.
- **Fix:** Quote the simulated power under the actual analysis. Specify C5 as a ratio of sums (losses / non-key trainer battles) resampled by run cluster, never as a per-battle binomial CI. Add a verdict for an upper bound below 0, and attribute the expected direction to the package, not to one flag.

### S7 [SHOULD-FIX, VERIFIED]

- **Design:** "Its gate runs on synthetic runs. A planted −0.2 effect must read LOWER, and a planted +0.2 HIGHER. A null must read LOWER in at most α of 200 synthetic seeds." (:124-125)
- **Evidence:** A ±0.2 effect is about 1.5-2x the leaders MDD (.10-.14) and 2.5-3x the rivals and Magma/Aqua MDDs (s14review/mdd.py), so any reasonable procedure passes. The error that decides C2-C4 is reading NOT LOWER when the truth is exactly −δ, and the gate never plants −δ. With 200 seeds, a procedure whose LOWER rate is .05 (twice the nominal .025) still passes '≤ α' (≤10 of 200 at α=.05) in roughly half of gate runs. My simulation of the design's bootstrap at −δ read NOT LOWER at .033 / .037 / .040 (leaders / rivals / Magma/Aqua; nominal .025; 300 seeds).
- **Problem:** The gate passes the procedure without testing the property the acceptance verdict depends on.
- **Fix:** Add gate rows that plant exactly −δ per group on synthetic data with the real structure: same-species-PID clusters, a random lane that shares clusters, and about 8 runs per lane. Require NOT LOWER ≤ .025 plus Monte Carlo error over at least 1,000 seeds. Plant 0 and check that P(NOT LOWER) matches the power the design states. Name the α.

### S9 [SHOULD-FIX, VERIFIED]

- **Design:** "STOP-3, a new failure ... ⇒ park the batch and diagnose with a driver. The batch is not a debugger." (:147-149); T2(d) "whether the run ended or was cut at the deadline" (:121-122); "Not a stop: B is slower than A" (:150); "The 1 h read also ... restates the expected n." (:151-152)
- **Evidence:** Nowhere in §4-§6 does the design say: whether a STOP-3 park voids C1-C5; whether a restarted batch is pooled with the parked one; how deadline-cut runs enter C1-C5; or whether the 1 h throughput read may change δ, --minutes or the analysis. x4_headless.py:1093-1094 stops every lane at the deadline, so every lane ends on a run in progress (batch0927 FINAL :345: 4 of 35 runs were in progress). D-funnel's "runs that beat X / runs started" (:199) counts a cut run as started but not beaten, which penalizes the slower arm, and the design expects B may be slower.
- **Problem:** A stop that depends on outcomes, with no pre-registered consequence, is optional stopping. Censoring and pooling rules chosen after the data are seen are a forking path, and the cut-run rule is exactly where arm-dependent run length becomes bias.
- **Fix:** Pre-register the following. Any STOP means C1-C5 are void and become descriptive. A restart is a new batch with a new profile tag and is never pooled. A deadline-cut run contributes only the first attempts it completed. Funnel denominators exclude runs cut before reaching X. A user park before a stated number of hours means the confirmatory verdicts carry the MDD for the realized n. The 1 h read may not change δ, --minutes or any analysis choice.

### S10 [NIT, VERIFIED]

- **Design:** "The per-trainer FIRST-attempt win rate" (:181), and C1's "the FIRST attempt of each key battle" (:175)
- **Evidence:** trainer_identity.py:179-180: is_first_fight is "True when the trainer's defeated flag (0x500 + id) is unset — read at battle START (it is set after a win)". The `first=` field of `[TRAINER] start` is therefore True on every lost re-attempt too.

## Angle `machinery`

**Verdict.** The runner side can be built and run as designed. T1 is a set of local changes to x4_headless.py: the Lane object carries its arm env, lane_env(lane) builds the environment, and check_headers already reads each profile's live logs. Every run writes a fresh log whose header is written before the reset. That header, env-all included, is about 1.2 KB, well inside the 8 KiB read. 8 lanes is well within this machine's capacity. I found no BLOCKER for the runner. The most important problem is that the batch's safety net does not work as written. The interim reads are told to look in the main checkout's x4-runs/<stamp>/, but x4_headless copies debug logs there only after the deadline. So at 1 h, 4 h and 8 h, STOP-1 passes on an empty set and STOP-2 would stop a healthy batch. STOP-3 also requires the run-end text to name DBLITEM/KIT/topoff/DEXCATCH/CATCH, and no derail message in the code carries those words (0 of 2,349 past run-end outcomes do), so it can never fire. Two further gaps the design missed: the MANIFEST (arms, sha256) is written only at the end, and the runner's main loop has no try/finally, so if x4_headless dies the 8 lanes keep running with no time limit. Separately, the SessionStart hook auto-pulls the main checkout whenever it is clean, which would change the frozen code mid-batch.
