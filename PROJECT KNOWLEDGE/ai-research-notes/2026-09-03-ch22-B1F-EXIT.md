## 3. ⛔ CORRECTED — THE EXIT COSTS **THREE** BATTLES, NOT FIVE. (My first answer was wrong.)

> ⛔⛔ **THIS SECTION ORIGINALLY READ "THE EXIT COSTS FIVE BATTLES" AND WAS COMMITTED THAT WAY.**
> An adversarial refuter caught it and I then re-derived it myself. **The minimum is THREE.**
> The error was methodological, not arithmetic: I asked a BINARY question — *"is there a
> trainer-free route?"* — got "no", and fell back to the SHORTEST route, which costs five. The right
> question was never "is there a free route" but **"what is the CHEAPEST route"**, i.e. a Dijkstra
> weighted by battles. ⇒ **when the ideal is unavailable, MINIMISE — do not default to the metric you
> happened to have.**
> ‼ And my BFS was wrong in a second way that made it pessimistic: **it ignored B1F's six
> `MB_SLIDE_SOUTH` tiles**, which actually HELP — they carry the player south for free, past sight
> lines, without a step of their own.

**✅ MY INDEPENDENTLY RE-DERIVED MINIMUM (battle-weighted Dijkstra, slides modelled):
3 battles / 19 tiles — Andrea, Bethany, Connie.**
```
(8,6)(8,7)(8,8)(8,9)(8,10)→slide→(8,12)(7,12)(6,12)(5,12)(5,13)(5,14)
```

*(The refuter independently found a different 3-battle route — {Tiffany, Andrea, Connie}, 35 tiles.

For contrast, the naive shortest walk is 19 tiles and **5** battles — which is what my first pass

  ⇒ a plain 4-connected walk model is WRONG on B1F, and wrong in the pessimistic direction.

  tiles. The 3-battle route above touches none.
* The ladder (11,22) is the map's only non-normal walkable behaviour (`0x61`, MB_LADDER).

## 3b. THE ORIGINAL (WRONG) FINDING, KEPT FOR THE RECORD

from this fall point. *(By contrast, had the bot been able to fall at (8,19), a 7-step trainer-free
route existed — which is exactly why the unreachable "best" was so tempting.)*

**✅ THE SIGHT-LINE RULE IS DECOMP-VERIFIED, NOT ASSUMED** (`src/trainer_see.c`):
`GetTrainerApproachDistanceSouth` is `trainer.x == player.x && y > trainer.y && y <= trainer.y +
range` — i.e. a trainer sees exactly `range` tiles along its facing — and the other three directions
mirror it. `CheckPathBetweenTrainerAndPlayer` then walks the intervening tiles and returns 0 if any
has a blocking collision, so **walls block sight**. My computation implements exactly this.
